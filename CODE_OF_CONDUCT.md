# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We commit to making participation in Commons Identity a harassment-free experience regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience level, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or other protected characteristic.

An identity project must be especially careful not to demand legal names, unnecessary personal history, Credentials, employer status, or proof of belonging as the price of participation. Use a participant's chosen name and pronouns. Do not correlate pseudonymous identities across spaces.

## Expected behavior

- Be specific, evidence-led, and kind when disagreeing.
- Critique ideas and changes, not people's worth or motives.
- Respect privacy, consent, boundaries, and requests to stop contact.
- Give credit and clearly mark adapted work.
- Make room for newcomers and people affected by identity-system failures.
- Disclose material conflicts of interest.
- Accept proportionate moderation and repair harm where possible.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, sexualized attention, or discriminatory language.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information, identity links, contact details, Credentials, keys, or recovery material.
- Deliberately attempting to deanonymize or correlate a participant.
- Impersonation, deceptive consent, coercion, or credential/status-based exclusion unrelated to project safety.
- Sustained disruption, bad-faith process manipulation, or retaliation for a report.
- Exploit details or secrets posted publicly against the security policy.
- Conduct that would reasonably be inappropriate in a professional community.

## Scope

This Code applies in project repositories, discussions, reviews, events, private project communication, and when someone officially represents the project. It also applies when off-project conduct creates a credible safety risk for participants.

## Reporting

For non-sensitive incidents, contact a maintainer through a GitHub moderation report or a minimal public Issue. For confidential reports, use the repository's [private reporting form](https://github.com/NishizukaKoichi/commons-identity/security/advisories/new), label the title “Code of Conduct,” and do not include unrelated vulnerability data.

Include only what is needed: what happened, where, when, relevant links, immediate safety concerns, and desired boundaries. Do not investigate other participants or collect unnecessary personal data.

Maintainers will limit access to people needed for response, consider conflicts and recusal, and protect reporter confidentiality as far as practical. They may be unable to keep information confidential when required for immediate safety or law, and will explain that constraint when possible.

## Enforcement

Responses are based on context, impact, pattern, and safety—not popularity, employer, funding, or contribution volume. Possible actions include:

1. **Correction:** private or public clarification and request to change behavior.
2. **Warning:** documented boundaries and consequences for repetition.
3. **Temporary restriction:** time-limited removal from interaction or project spaces.
4. **Permanent restriction:** removal from project spaces and representative roles.

Maintainers may remove harmful content, rotate exposed secrets, or temporarily lock a conversation before completing review. Retaliation or repeated boundary violations increase severity. An appeal must identify a factual error, conflict, process failure, or materially new evidence.
